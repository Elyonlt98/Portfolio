About

I'm a mechanical engineer working on a hard materials problem: joining silicon carbide reliably enough to use in high-temperature assemblies. My PhD work at UT Dallas covers the whole chain, from designing joining compositions to automating the laser process and testing the joints.

Before the PhD, I spent two years developing medical devices in an FDA-regulated, ISO 13485 environment for clients including Baxter and Smith & Nephew. That taught me to design for manufacturing and to document from day one, habits I now apply to research.

Current research

Laser-assisted joining of silicon carbide (SiC) ceramics for high-temperature structural applications, with semiconductor equipment, aerospace and defense, and industrial heat exchange in mind.

What I'm responsible for

  • Built and run the diode laser joining setup with an industrial robot, including I/O integration and motion programming.
  • Develop joining compositions through DOE, screening for wetting, bond quality and thermal stability.
  • Link process parameters to joint strength and failure modes through mechanical testing and fractography.
  • Keep experiments traceable and reproducible with design-control style documentation.
