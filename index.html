About

I am a mechanical engineer and PhD researcher at UT Dallas, joining silicon carbide ceramics reliably enough for high-temperature assemblies.

My doctoral work spans the full process chain, from the design of joining compositions to automation of the laser process and mechanical characterization of the joints.

Before beginning my PhD, I spent two years developing medical devices in an FDA-regulated, ISO 13485 environment for clients including Baxter and Smith & Nephew. That experience instilled a disciplined approach to design for manufacturing and rigorous documentation, which I now apply to my research.

Current research

My PhD research develops a laser-assisted process for joining silicon carbide (SiC) ceramics for high-temperature structural applications, including semiconductor equipment, aerospace and defense, and industrial heat exchangers.

ProjectLaser-assisted joining of SiC ceramics
LabThe University of Texas at Dallas
ProgramPhD, Mechanical Engineering
StatusIn progress, 2026 to 2030

Key responsibilities

  • Set up and operate the diode laser joining system with an industrial robot, including I/O integration and motion programming.
  • Develop joining compositions through DOE, screening for wetting, bond quality and thermal stability.
  • Link process parameters to joint strength and failure modes through mechanical testing and fractography.
  • Keep experiments traceable and reproducible using design-control-style documentation.
